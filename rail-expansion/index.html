What happens when
railway tracks heat up?

Steel rails expand when they heat up. If there is not enough space between them, they can push against each other, building up pressure and possibly bending, buckling or becoming damaged.

Four things that can happen when rails heat up

①

Rails expand

Metal expands as it heats up, so each rail becomes longer.

②

Gaps narrow

The space between the rails becomes smaller.

③

Rails push together

Once the gaps close, the rails cannot expand freely.

④

Buckling and damage

If compression becomes too great, rails may bend sideways and create a danger.

Will the tracks explode?

Usually, tracks do not explode like a bomb. A more accurate term is track buckling: when heated rails cannot expand freely, they may suddenly bend or move sideways. In severe cases, this can damage the track and affect train safety. The animation exaggerates the bending so it is easier to see.

Do modern railways always have gaps?

Not always. Many modern railways use continuously welded rail. Engineers manage thermal stress through fastenings, installation temperature and track design, so there does not have to be a visible gap between every section. The important point is that railways must be designed to manage thermal expansion and contraction.
