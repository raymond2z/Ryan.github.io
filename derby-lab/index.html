Model, data & limitations ＋

Your actual track

The running surface is sampled from pinewood derby.3mf at y = 26.875 mm in the first lane, every 2 mm. The model uses all eight V3 joints. Two lanes are shown; physics is simulated longitudinally along one track.

What is simulated

Gravity, wheel inertia, curvature-dependent normal force, rolling and axle friction, air drag, and joint losses. The car enters ballistic flight when contact force reaches zero. Landing uses adjustable collision parameters. Fixed 1 ms time step; the contact profile is smoothed over about 20 mm.

An engineering approximation

The car is represented by one equivalent contact point. Body attitude and wheelbase are illustrative. Bottoming out, sequential front/rear wheel contact, tire deformation, lateral drift, guide-rail collisions, and structural strength are not modeled. Flight and impact results depend on these assumptions and need physical calibration. No finish probability is predicted.

Reading the comparisons

Finish time is when the car reference point crosses the finish plane. Stalled runs have no finish time; airborne finishes are labeled separately. Search ranks grounded finishes first, then less airtime, then shorter finish time. This is not a safety or race-rule certification.
