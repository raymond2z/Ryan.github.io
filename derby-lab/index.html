How these bars are calculated

All bars use the same fixed scale for this run. Percentages refer to starting energy, including an initial push. Potential energy uses the model reference point above the lowest track or sampled car position. Kinetic energy includes translation (½mv²) and wheel rotation (½Iω²).

Dissipation accumulates rolling and axle resistance, air drag, joint losses and estimated impact losses. Wheels retain their spin in flight and synchronize on landing. The signed balance residual is shown separately: numerical integration and simplified contact / spin synchronization can introduce a mismatch. It is not labeled as friction. Assumptions and how to read the result

This is a centered first-lane, rigid chassis fit check. Circular wheels are supported on an unsmoothed road envelope extracted from the original mesh; the axle separation is fixed. The underside is flat and centered between the axles. Guide height comes from the mesh, including its curved sections. Ground clearance is measured on a flat road; it is different from center-of-gravity height.

The scan uses 2 mm rear-axle steps and a 0.5 mm wheel-center lookup. Conservative envelopes use road sections at y = ±18, ±20, ±24, ±30 mm and guide sections at y = −16, −8, 0, 8, 16 mm. The gap is measured normal to the chassis underside. Positive sampled clearance is not a collision certificate. Unsampled poses, printing tolerances, steering, tire deformation, moving impacts and pitch dynamics are not included. Track tilt rigidly rotates the assembly and does not change this static fit result.

Shortening the wheelbase reduces the span across a crest, but increases overhang when body length stays fixed. Compare both effects. A negative result is an impossible supported pose: it flags interference rather than simulating what happens after contact. The scan excludes incomplete start/end support; the race animation can still pass through these locations because it uses a separate approximation.

Buttons show sampled intervals of at least 5 ms (up to 20). The contact bands retain shorter changes; facet transitions can cause numerical contact chatter. Playback samples are 1 ms apart.

Two-axle model, starting line and limits

Each axle represents its left/right wheel pair in a longitudinal plane. A rigid body has independent horizontal and vertical velocity, pitch angle and angular velocity; I = total mass × pitch radius of gyration². Wheel rotation is tracked separately per axle. CG height is above the axle line, not chassis ground clearance. Dimensions use the chassis fit controls above. CG and inertia are demonstration values saved in this browser; export the pitch report to retain these settings and sampled telemetry. Existing setup JSON and share links do not include CG / inertia.

The run starts at the first placement with both wheels and the whole chassis inside the original track. Initial speed is along the starting axle line. Finish means the front bumper crosses the end plane. This is a different timing interval from the equivalent-contact race above. A chassis / guide penetration beyond 0.2 mm stops the simulation; no scraping or chassis-impact response is invented.

Unilateral contact impulses prevent wheels pulling on the road. Ideal rolling couples axle translation and wheel spin; drag, rolling resistance, axle torque and normal landing restitution use the current car setup. Joint-loss percentage and landing-speed retention belong only to the original model and are not applied here. No tire slip limit, suspension, lateral motion, steering, guide-side collision or wheel gyroscopic torque is modeled. Road and guide profiles use the unsmoothed 3MF envelopes from the fit check.

The solver uses a 0.2 ms timestep and 32 contact iterations. Potential energy follows the actual CG; kinetic energy includes translation, wheel spin and body pitch. Dissipation tracks resistance and resolved impacts; constraint / integration error remains a signed residual. A wheel penetration over 1 mm or residual over the larger of 0.002 J / 3% of starting energy marks the run numerically invalid. Physical experiments are still needed to calibrate the parameters.

Model, data & limitations ＋

Your actual track

The running surface is sampled from pinewood derby.3mf at y = 26.875 mm in the first lane, every 2 mm. The model uses all eight V3 joints. Two lanes are shown; physics is simulated longitudinally along one track.

What is simulated

Gravity, wheel inertia, curvature-dependent normal force, rolling and axle friction, air drag, and joint losses. The car enters ballistic flight when contact force reaches zero. Landing uses adjustable collision parameters. Fixed 1 ms time step; the contact profile is smoothed over about 20 mm.

An engineering approximation

The car is represented by one equivalent contact point. Vehicle dimensions also feed the separate static fit check. Race attitude is illustrative. Dynamic bottoming out, sequential front/rear wheel contact, tire deformation, lateral drift, guide-rail collisions, and structural strength are not modeled. Flight and impact results depend on these assumptions and need physical calibration. No finish probability is predicted.

Reading the comparisons

Finish time is when the car reference point crosses the finish plane. Stalled runs have no finish time; airborne finishes are labeled separately. Search ranks eligible setups by finish time. Fastest finish permits airborne finishes; stay-on-track requires no contact loss, including micro-hops; impact-limited search requires a grounded finish and all landing normal speeds within the chosen limit. No qualifying result is reported as a winner. This is not a safety or race-rule certification.
